# Packet R — Research desk report

Audited 20 September 2026. This report covers all 28 quotations in `data/texts.json`, the 16 displayed period fields, and the three Egypt-related credibility issues called out in Packet R.

## Verification standard

`verified: true` means the displayed English wording was located in a named translation, edition, or first-party transcript. A similar idea, an unattributed quotation-site match, a modern adaptation, or wording that differs from the named primary edition does not pass. “Primary text” below means the work in which the line is claimed to occur; for modern spoken teachers, it means a first-party archive or a named interview. Exactness here verifies the English edition, not the philosophical faithfulness of a translation. That distinction matters especially for Thomas Byrom’s very free *Dhammapada*.

Result: **16 verified; 12 unverified.** No quotation wording was changed, because the content verifier treats the legacy wording as fixed. Corrections and proposed replacements therefore appear only in source notes and this report.

## Quote-by-quote findings

### 1. The Upanishads

#### 1.1 “As a person acts, so he becomes in life …”

- **Verdict:** verified.
- **Edition and location:** Eknath Easwaran, *The Upanishads*, 2nd ed. (Nilgiri Press, 2007), Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 4.4.5.
- **Located passage:** “As a person acts, so he becomes in life. Those who do good become good; those who do harm become bad.”
- **Evidence:** the sentence is present in the [Easwaran edition scan](https://klinikong.com/spirituality/Upanishads-EknathEaswaran.pdf). It is the translator’s English, not a free-floating “ancient proverb.”

#### 1.2 “From the unreal lead me to the real …”

- **Verdict:** verified.
- **Edition and location:** Eduard Röer, trans., *The Brihad Aranyaka Upanishad and the Commentary of Sankara Acharya on Its First Chapter* (Bibliotheca Indica, 1856), 1.3.28, First Chapter, Third Brahmana, p. 59.
- **Located passage:** “From the unreal lead me to the real, from darkness lead me to light, from death lead me to immortality.”
- **Evidence:** exact wording in the [1856 edition scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/50/%28i%29_Bibliotheca_Indica%3B_Collection_of_Oriental_Works_published_under_the_Partonage_of_the_Don._Court_of_Directors_of_the_East_India_Company_and_the_superintendence_of_Asiatic_Society_of_Bengal_%28IA_dli.granth.41076%29.pdf); the Government of Maharashtra’s [catalog record](https://granthsanjeevani.com/jspui/handle/123456789/41076) identifies Röer, title, publisher, and date.

#### 1.3 “You are what your deep, driving desire is …”

- **Verdict:** verified.
- **Edition and location:** Eknath Easwaran, *The Upanishads*, 2nd ed. (Nilgiri Press, 2007), Brihadaranyaka Upanishad 4.4.5.
- **Located passage:** “You are what your deep, driving desire is. As your desire is, so is your will.”
- **Evidence:** exact wording in the [Easwaran edition scan](https://klinikong.com/spirituality/Upanishads-EknathEaswaran.pdf).

### 2. Tao Te Ching

#### 2.1 “The Tao that can be named is not the eternal Tao.”

- **Verdict:** unverified.
- **Comparison edition and location:** D. C. Lau, *Tao Te Ching* (Penguin, 1963), chapter 1.
- **Located passage:** Lau begins, “The way that can be spoken of / Is not the constant way; / The name that can be named / Is not the constant name.”
- **Finding:** the catalog sentence splices the subject of the first clause (“way/Tao”) to the verb and predicate of the second (“named … constant name”). Chapter 1 does not say this exact English sentence. A [multi-translation chapter 1 sheet](https://instruct.uwo.ca/philosophy/154e/Taoteching.pdf) shows the same two-clause structure across named editions. Suggested replacement: Lau’s first two lines above.

#### 2.2 “When I let go of what I am, I become what I might be.”

- **Verdict:** unverified; modern adaptation, not *Tao Te Ching*.
- **Actual source located:** John Heider, *The Tao of Leadership* (Humanics, 1985), section 22, “The Paradox of Letting Go,” p. 43.
- **Located passage:** “By letting go, I become myself.” Heider’s prose in the same adaptation supplies the circulated “what I am / what I might be” formulation.
- **Finding:** no classical Chinese line or named *Tao Te Ching* translation supports the catalog wording. It belongs to Heider’s twentieth-century leadership adaptation; a searchable [edition copy](https://pdfcoffee.com/the-tao-of-leadership-john-heider-pdf-free.html) locates it. Suggested genuine replacement: James Legge, chapter 22, “The partial becomes complete; the crooked, straight,” available with the Chinese text at the [Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/dao-de-jing).

#### 2.3 “Nature does not hurry, yet everything is accomplished.”

- **Verdict:** unverified; modern summary of *wu wei*.
- **Comparison edition and location:** James Legge, *The Tao Te Ching*, chapter 37.
- **Located passage:** “The Tao in its regular course does nothing … and so there is nothing which it does not do.”
- **Finding:** neither the Chinese text nor the named historical translations contain “Nature does not hurry.” The [Quote Investigator-style textual audit](https://laozi-taoteching.org/en/quotecheck/nature-no-hurry/) traces the mismatch, and the [Chinese Text Project](https://ctext.org/dao-de-jing) supplies the actual chapter text. Suggested replacement: the chapter 37 sentence above.

### 3. Bhagavad Gita

#### 3.1 “You have a right to perform your prescribed duty …”

- **Verdict:** verified.
- **Edition and location:** A. C. Bhaktivedanta Swami Prabhupada, *Bhagavad-gita As It Is*, 2nd ed. (Bhaktivedanta Book Trust, 1983), 2.47.
- **Located passage:** “You have a right to perform your prescribed duty, but you are not entitled to the fruits of action.”
- **Evidence:** exact opening in the publisher tradition’s [Vedabase text of 2.47](https://vedabase.cc/bg/2/47/).

#### 3.2 “The wise see that there is action in the midst of inaction …”

- **Verdict:** verified.
- **Edition and location:** Eknath Easwaran, *The Bhagavad Gita*, 2nd ed. (Nilgiri Press, 2007), 4.18.
- **Located passage:** “The wise see that there is action in the midst of inaction and inaction in the midst of action.”
- **Evidence:** exact sentence in the searchable [Easwaran edition copy](https://www.shivbabas.org/_files/ugd/8cc233_dff834bb8df44594aa6545692329458b.pdf?index=true).

### 4. The Dhammapada

#### 4.1 “All things arise and pass away. But the awakened awake forever.”

- **Verdict:** verified as Byrom’s English, with a major fidelity warning.
- **Edition and location:** Thomas Byrom, *The Dhammapada: The Sayings of the Buddha* (Wildwood House, 1976), chapter 18, “Impurity,” corresponding loosely to verses 254–255.
- **Located passage:** “All things arise and pass away. But the awakened awake forever.”
- **Evidence:** exact wording in the [Byrom edition scan](https://davar.net/FILE/Dhammapada_(Buddha_Gautama_5th_Century_BC_Thomas_Byrom_1976).pdf). A closer Pali translation by Acharya Buddharakkhita says that conditioned things are not eternal and that the awakened do not waver; see [verses 254–255 in the BPS edition](https://www.bps.lk/olib/bp/bp203s_Buddharakkhita_Dhammapada.pdf). Thus this is traceable to a named edition, but should not be presented as a literal Pali-canon sentence.

#### 4.2 “We are what we think. All that we are arises with our thoughts.”

- **Verdict:** verified as Byrom’s English, again not literal Pali.
- **Edition and location:** Thomas Byrom, *The Dhammapada* (1976), chapter 1, “Choices,” verses 1–2.
- **Located passage:** “We are what we think. All that we are arises with our thoughts.”
- **Evidence:** exact wording in the [Byrom edition scan](https://davar.net/FILE/Dhammapada_(Buddha_Gautama_5th_Century_BC_Thomas_Byrom_1976).pdf). Buddharakkhita’s closer rendering begins “Mind precedes all mental states”; see the [canonical comparison](https://vipassana.com/canon/khuddaka/dhp1/1.html). The catalog line is a named modern rendering, not a word-for-word saying recoverable from Pali.

### 5. Plato

#### 5.1 “The prison-house is the world of sight …”

- **Verdict:** verified.
- **Edition and location:** Benjamin Jowett, *The Republic of Plato*, 3rd ed. (Oxford University Press, 1892), Republic 7.517b.
- **Located passage:** “the prison-house is the world of sight, the light of the fire is the sun.”
- **Evidence:** exact clause in the [Jowett text of Republic VII](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/republic.8.vii.html).

#### 5.2 “The unexamined life is not worth living.”

- **Verdict:** verified as Plato, but filed under the wrong dialogue.
- **Edition and location:** Benjamin Jowett, *The Dialogues of Plato*, 3rd ed. (Oxford University Press, 1892), *Apology* 38a—not *Republic*.
- **Located passage:** “the unexamined life is not worth living.”
- **Evidence:** exact clause in the [Jowett text of Apology](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/apology.html). The data note now records the cataloguing error without changing layout/content fields.

### 6. Marcus Aurelius, Meditations

#### 6.1 “You have power over your mind—not outside events …”

- **Verdict:** unverified; anonymous modern Stoic paraphrase.
- **Comparison edition and location:** George Long, *The Thoughts of the Emperor M. Aurelius Antoninus* (1862), 8.47.
- **Located passage:** “If thou art pained by any external thing, it is not this thing that disturbs thee, but thy own judgment about it.”
- **Finding:** standard translations do not contain the catalog wording. A [source audit](https://trylumoro.com/verified/marcus-aurelius-power-over-mind) reaches the same negative result; the genuine Long passage is available at [Lexundria, 8.47](https://lexundria.com/m_aur_med/8.47/lg). Suggested replacement: Long 8.47 above.

#### 6.2 “Everything we hear is an opinion, not a fact …”

- **Verdict:** unverified; modern expansion.
- **Comparison edition and location:** George Long, *The Thoughts of the Emperor M. Aurelius Antoninus* (1862), 2.15.
- **Located passage:** “Everything is opinion.”
- **Finding:** no section in *Meditations* contains the two balanced English sentences. A [fact check](https://dailycaller.com/2019/10/15/fact-check-marcus-aurelius-opinion-fact-perspective-truth-quote/) documents the failed attribution, while [Long 2.15](https://lexundria.com/m_aur_med/2.15/lg) supplies the much shorter genuine thought. Suggested replacement: “Everything is opinion.”

### 8. Rumi

#### 8.1 “You were born with wings, why prefer to crawl through life?”

- **Verdict:** unverified; modern attribution.
- **Actual trail located:** the earliest named print occurrence found was Larry Chang, *Wisdom for the Soul* (2006), p. 26, where it is already attributed to Rumi without Persian text, poem number, or translator.
- **Finding:** no Persian original or named Rumi translation was located. The bibliographic trail is summarized by [LibQuotes](https://libquotes.com/rumi/quote/lbc8x6r), but it does not establish Rumi as author. Suggested genuine replacement: R. A. Nicholson, *Masnavi* III.1540–1541, where “the bird, your soul, will be distressed in its cage” and seeks escape; see the [Nicholson text with Persian](https://www.masnavi.net/3/25/eng/1/1539/).

#### 8.2 “Yesterday I was clever … Today I am wise …”

- **Verdict:** unverified; viral modern attribution.
- **Primary-text result:** no poem number, Persian source, or named translation was located, including in the principal Nicholson and Arberry translations and Coleman Barks anthologies.
- **Finding:** the available audit evidence indicates that Persian versions online are back-translations from the viral English; see the [provenance review](https://pastwisdom.com/authors/rumi/quotes/yesterday-clever-so-wanted-change-2a39). No replacement is proposed because no passage in the same text can be responsibly identified as this sentence’s source. That is stronger than guessing at a thematically similar Rumi line.

## Egyptian credibility checks

These items are not quotation objects in `data/texts.json`, so no data field was changed. They are recorded because Packet R specifically requires the audit and because they affect the site’s wider credibility layer.

### “A light heart travels far”

No primary Egyptian text, spell number, papyrus, Egyptologist, or named translation was located. It should be treated as a modern faux-Egyptian proverb, not evidence about the weighing of the heart. A genuine, relevant replacement is Book of the Dead spell 30B: “O my heart which I had from my mother … do not stand up as a witness against me,” in R. O. Faulkner’s translation. The [British Museum record for the Papyrus of Ani heart-scarab vignette](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA10470-3) supplies the object context; the translation is available in this [Book of the Dead edition](https://palimpsest.stmarytx.edu/thanneken/th7391/primary/EgyptianBookOfTheDead%281200bce%29.pdf).

### The Kybalion

*The Kybalion* is not ancient Egyptian and is not an ancient Hermetic source. It is a Chicago New Thought book first published in **1908** under the pseudonym “Three Initiates,” with authorship commonly associated with William Walker Atkinson. The [1908 edition scan](https://www.yogebooks.com/english/atkinson/1908kybalion.pdf) establishes the publication, while Philip Deslippe’s [critical edition record](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Kybalion.html?id=tKbWObPHouMC) documents the modern authorship problem. Any site copy calling its seven principles “ancient Egyptian teachings” should be corrected in a later, explicitly scoped packet.

### The Emerald Tablet

The *Emerald Tablet* is Hermetic, but the surviving textual history is **Arabic-era**, not pharaonic Egyptian. The earliest known Arabic recensions belong to roughly the late eighth or early ninth century CE and were later translated into Latin. A scholarly open-access study, [“The Emerald Tablet: The Earliest Textual Witnesses”](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/9783110677263-009/pdf?licenseType=open-access), lays out the Arabic witnesses. The [Sacred Texts historical overview](https://sacred-texts.com/alc/emerald.htm) supplies the later translation tradition. It must not be dated as an ancient Egyptian inscription.

## Bottom line

The strongest problems are not marginal punctuation disputes. Eleven catalog items are either modern paraphrases/misattributions or altered composites; the twelfth (Tolle) fails only because a word was dropped from the named edition.
